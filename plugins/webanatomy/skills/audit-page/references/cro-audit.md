# CRO Audit Lens (for free recommendations)

This is the lens for the RECOMMENDATIONS, and it is deliberately separate from the
scoring rubric (`scoring.md`). The rubric produces the score — the facts and the
proof the page needs work. This produces the recommendations — a free, expert
conversion audit.

**Do not derive recommendations from which rubric items passed or failed.** That
path gives detailed-but-mediocre, checkbox-grade output. Instead, read the page as
a sharp CRO expert and surface the highest-leverage moves, prioritized by impact.
The quality bar is a strong teardown (specific, opinionated, ROI-ordered), not a
checklist readout.

## How to think (impact order)

Audit across these, roughly in order of conversion impact. Use them to think, not
to tick:

1. **Value proposition clarity** — In 5 seconds, does a visitor get what this is,
   the outcome, who it's for, and why this over the alternative? This is the
   highest-leverage area; weak value prop caps everything below it.
2. **Headline & message** — Does the H1 promise an outcome (not just name the
   product)? Does it match the traffic source's promise (ad → page)?
3. **CTA** — One clear primary action? Value-loaded copy (not "Submit"/"Être
   rappelé")? Consistent across the page? Visible at the decision points? Risk
   reduced right beside it?
4. **Visual hierarchy & scannability** — Can a scanner get the message? Is the
   most important thing the most prominent? Above-the-fold focus.
5. **Trust & social proof** — The right proof, framed, placed near the decision
   (logos, quantified results, named testimonials, security/compliance, awards).
6. **Objection handling** — Are the real buyer objections answered before the ask
   (price, risk, fit, time-to-value, switching, "is it really free")? FAQ?
7. **Friction** — Form length, unclear next step, competing actions, missing
   reassurance, slow or broken elements.

## Be free, go beyond the list

You are not limited to those seven. Surface anything high-leverage the page
reveals: structural fixes (no form / no conversion path), message-match gaps,
sector-specific compliance (e.g. capital-loss risk notice + consent on a regulated
finance page), narrative order, page focus. If it would move conversion and you
can defend it, recommend it. Judgment beats coverage.

## Page-type emphasis

- **Landing page (paid):** message-match with the ad, single CTA, complete
  argument on one page, remove distractions.
- **Homepage:** clear positioning for cold visitors, fast path to the main action,
  self-selection for different audiences.
- **Pricing:** plan clarity, a recommended plan, objection handling, easy path to
  checkout.
- **Feature:** feature → benefit, use cases, comparison, clear CTA to try/buy.

## Output the recommendations

- **One recommendation per distinct issue.** Do not bundle (no-outcome-headline,
  no-named-audience, no-risk-reducer are three recs, not one). Be comprehensive.
- **Prioritize by impact, not by any score:** HIGH (blocks comprehension/trust/
  conversion), MEDIUM (meaningful lift), LOW (polish). At most two or three HIGHs
  — name the single highest-leverage fix.
- **Type each rec by the fix it needs:** `copy` (wording, message, angle; the fix
  is rewrite alternatives) or `design` (structure, layout, hierarchy, visual
  proof; the fix is benchmark-grounded section references).
- **Each rec is a concrete move:** what to add, remove, rewrite, or re-order, tied
  to what is actually on the page. "Move a named customer quote directly under the
  hero CTA" beats "improve trust."
- Tag each with its section. Plain language only — no rubric item IDs, no scores.
- Frame as opportunities (the gain), not complaints, and follow the shared house
  style for the written findings ("The X…" not "Your X…", no em-dashes, severity
  vocabulary). The canon is `webanatomy-setup/references/house-style.md`.

The score (from the rubric) and these recommendations are generated independently.
They meet only in the output: score on top as the proof, recommendations below as
the substance.
